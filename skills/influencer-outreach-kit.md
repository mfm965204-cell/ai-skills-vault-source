# Influencer Outreach Kit

Creator campaigns fail in the same three places: the list is whoever replied
first, the brief is a product link, and nobody wrote down who owns the video
afterwards. The result is spend with no attribution and footage you cannot
reuse.

This skill gives you the paperwork and the cadence: a qualified list with a
stated audience match, a budget built from a rate card rather than a hunch,
three outreach touches over five working days, a brief with a shot list, and a
contract covering usage rights, revisions and disclosure. Every deliverable is
a document you can send today.

## When to use this skill

- You have a product launch in four weeks and a budget between ₹50,000 and ₹5,00,000 for creators.
- A previous campaign produced views and no way to tell whether any sale came from it.
- A creator posted without the paid-partnership label and you need the next brief to be compliant.
- You want to reuse creator footage in ads and have no idea whether you are allowed to.

Do NOT use for: paid search or performance ads (different auction and
measurement), or a celebrity endorsement deal that needs legal review of
exclusivity and morality clauses.

## Stage 1 — Fix the goal and the one deliverable

A campaign with two goals measures neither. Pick one outcome and the one asset
that serves it.

1.1 Write the goal as a number with a date: "400 installs from creator links by 15 December" or "60 qualified leads with a WhatsApp opt-in".

1.2 Pick one deliverable format per creator — a 30-second reel, a 60-second YouTube integration, or a static carousel — and do not mix formats inside one brief.

1.3 Decide the conversion path before outreach: a tracked link, a discount code, or a landing page per creator. A creator post with no distinct path is unmeasurable.

Gate: one goal with a number and a date, one format, and one distinct
conversion path per creator.

## Stage 2 — Qualify the list before you write a single email

Audience match beats follower count, and engagement rate exposes bought
followers.

2.1 Build the list in a sheet with eight columns: handle, platform, followers, engagement rate, audience top-3 cities, content category, contact email, and last sponsored post date.

2.2 Set bands: 5,000 to 100,000 followers for micro creators, engagement above 3% on reels, audience top-3 cities matching your serviceable market.

2.3 Check for bought followers: a 60%+ follower growth in one month, or comments that are all emoji, means the audience is not real.

| Signal | Healthy range | Reject if |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement rate on reels | 3% to 8% | below 1.5% at 50,000+ followers |
| Comment quality | questions and specifics | only emoji or one-word replies |
| Audience geography | 60%+ in target cities | 70%+ outside India for an India offer |
| Sponsored post density | 1 in 5 posts or fewer | every post is an ad |
| Follower growth | steady | 60%+ jump in one month |

Gate: every row has all eight fields filled and no row fails a reject
condition.

## Stage 3 — Price from a rate card, not from the ask

The first number in the conversation is the anchor. Arrive with a range and a
reason.

3.1 Set a per-deliverable range by follower band: ₹2,500 to ₹6,000 for 5,000 to 20,000 followers, ₹6,000 to ₹18,000 for 20,000 to 50,000, ₹18,000 to ₹40,000 for 50,000 to 100,000 — ranges are estimates and vary by category.

3.2 Price the extras separately: paid usage rights add 25% to 50%, exclusivity for 30 days adds 20%, and a second revision beyond two is billed.

3.3 Reserve 15% of the budget for the two creators who over-deliver, rather than paying everyone the top of the band up front.

Gate: a written budget line per creator with the band, the deliverable and the
extras listed.

## Stage 4 — Send three touches over five working days

One email gets one reply from roughly one in eight qualified creators. The
follow-ups carry the campaign.

4.1 Touch one is short, specific and about them: name the post that made you shortlist them, state the deliverable and the fee range in the first four lines.

4.2 Touch two after two working days adds one new fact — the timeline, the product sample, or the brief — and repeats the ask in one sentence.

4.3 Touch three after three more days is a close: "I am locking the list on Friday; happy to include you if the brief works."

```text
Subject: 30s reel for <product>, ₹X, brief attached

Hi <name> — your reel on <specific post> is why I am writing: the way you
showed the <detail> is exactly what we need for <product>.

We are paying ₹X for one 30-second reel, brief attached, publishing
window 5-15 December. Two revisions included, paid usage rights for 30
days at +25% if you are open to it.

Want me to send the product and the shot list?
```

Gate: three touches logged per creator with dates, and every reply answered
within one working day.

## Stage 5 — Brief with a shot list, not a link

A creator who receives a product link makes the ad they would make for anyone.
A shot list makes it your ad.

5.1 Write five sections: the one-sentence message, three must-show moments, the hook in the first 2 seconds, the call to action, and the three things not to say.

5.2 Give the exact caption text, the disclosure line and the tracked link or code, in a copy-paste block.

5.3 State the deliverables: one 30-second reel in 9:16, the raw footage, and the caption; and the publish window with dates.

Gate: the brief fits one page, names the hook, the three must-show moments and
the caption verbatim.

## Stage 6 — Put the rights in writing before the shoot

Usage rights are the most expensive omission in creator marketing: re-cutting
a reel for ads without rights is a claim waiting to happen.

6.1 Contract four terms in plain language: deliverables with dates, two revisions, 30-day paid usage rights with an option to extend at 25%, and 30-day category exclusivity.

6.2 Payment terms 50% on signing and 50% within 7 days of the post going live, with the invoice and the UPI or bank details in the contract.

6.3 Add a content-removal clause: the post stays up 90 days minimum, and a takedown within that window triggers a partial refund.

Gate: a signed document per creator covering deliverables, revisions, usage,
exclusivity, payment and takedown.

## Stage 7 — Disclose the paid partnership correctly

Undisclosed paid promotion breaks Indian consumer-protection rules and
platform policy, and the creator carries the risk with you.

7.1 Require the platform's own paid-partnership label where it exists — Instagram's "Paid partnership" tag, YouTube's paid promotion checkbox.

7.2 Require a plain-language disclosure in the first two lines of the caption and in the video itself: "Paid partnership with <brand>", spoken or on screen.

7.3 Keep the disclosure out of a hashtag pile at the bottom; a disclosure a viewer has to scroll to find is not a disclosure.

Gate: every published post carries both the platform label and a visible
in-content disclosure.

## Stage 8 — Attribute the traffic with one path per creator

Without a distinct path, the campaign's contribution is a guess made from view
counts.

8.1 Give each creator a unique short link and, where the platform allows, a unique code; both land on the same page with a `utm_source=<handle>` parameter.

8.2 Track four numbers per creator: link clicks, conversion rate, cost per conversion, and the assisted conversions within 7 days.

8.3 Ask two creators for a whitelisting or Spark Ads permission, because paid amplification of the best organic post usually outperforms a second organic post.

Gate: every creator has a distinct link and code, and the dashboard shows cost
per conversion per creator.

## Stage 9 — Review, then repeat or stop

The second campaign is where the data pays. Decide with numbers, not
impressions.

9.1 Rank creators by cost per conversion, not by views, and identify the two to rebook at a higher fee.

9.2 Record the ones who missed the deadline, needed four revisions, or posted without disclosure on a shared blacklist sheet.

9.3 Write one page of what to change: the brief line that was ignored, the hook that worked, the price that was too high.

Gate: a ranked table with cost per conversion, a rebook list, and one page of
changes for the next round.

## Worked example - ₹1,80,000 across 9 creators

A skincare brand launched a ₹799 serum with 9 micro creators in three cities.

| Creator band | Count | Fee each | Total | Clicks | Conversions | Cost per conversion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5k to 20k | 4 | ₹4,000 | ₹16,000 | 1,120 | 74 | ₹216 |
| 20k to 50k | 3 | ₹12,000 | ₹36,000 | 2,880 | 121 | ₹298 |
| 50k to 100k | 2 | ₹26,000 | ₹52,000 | 3,900 | 96 | ₹542 |
| Usage rights | 3 creators | +25% | ₹13,000 | — | — | — |
| Paid amplification | 1 post | ₹63,000 | ₹63,000 | 4,410 | 209 | ₹301 |

Total spend ₹1,80,000 for 500 conversions at ₹360 blended, against a target of
₹400. The 5k to 20k band converted best per rupee, which is the usual shape:
smaller audiences buy more per view.

Two creators were the useful surprises. One in the 20k band produced 61
conversions from a single reel that used the product on camera for 11 seconds
rather than showing the bottle. That detail went into the next brief as a
must-show moment.

The failure was a 90k creator who posted without the paid-partnership label.
The post was reported, taken down on day three, and the fee was refunded under
the 90-day live clause. The fix was procedural: the disclosure moved into the
caption block that creators copy, and every post is checked within 24 hours of
publishing.

The tracking that made the table possible:

```text
https://brand.in/serum?utm_source=creatorhandle&utm_medium=reel&utm_campaign=launch-dec
code: CREATORHANDLE10  (10% off, 30-day expiry, one use per customer)
```

Without the per-creator code, three of the nine creators would have been
credited with each other's sales.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] One goal with a number and a date.
- [ ] One deliverable format per creator.
- [ ] A distinct conversion path per creator.
- [ ] List sheet has all eight qualifying columns.
- [ ] No creator fails a reject condition.
- [ ] Budget built from the rate card with extras priced.
- [ ] Three touches sent over five working days.
- [ ] Every reply answered within one working day.
- [ ] Brief fits one page with hook and three must-show moments.
- [ ] Contract covers deliverables, revisions, usage, exclusivity, payment, takedown.
- [ ] Platform paid-partnership label required.
- [ ] In-content disclosure in the first two lines.
- [ ] Unique link and code per creator, with UTM parameters.
- [ ] Cost per conversion computed per creator.
- [ ] Rebook list and blacklist updated after the campaign.

## Failure modes

| Symptom | Real cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Views high, sales flat | The post had no distinct link, so the traffic was unattributable | One short link and one code per creator, always |
| Creator posts something off-brand | The brief had a product link and no shot list | Send a one-page brief with the hook and the three must-show moments |
| Reel cannot be used in ads | Usage rights were never bought | Price paid usage at +25% to +50% in the contract before the shoot |
| Post removed after two weeks | No minimum live period in the contract | Add a 90-day live clause with a partial refund on early takedown |
| Disclosure missing on a paid post | The caption block was copy-pasted and the label skipped | Put the disclosure in the caption block and check the post on day one |
| Half the list never replies | One email, no follow-up, sent to a generic address | Three touches over five working days, to a named contact |
| Cost per conversion 4x the target | The audience was outside the serviceable market | Re-qualify on audience geography, not follower count |
