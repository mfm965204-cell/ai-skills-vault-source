# Resume & Job Hunter

Volume does not get jobs. Specificity does. This skill builds one strong narrative and aims it precisely.

## When to use this skill

- Applying for a role and getting no callbacks.
- Switching domain, level or country.
- Preparing for interviews and negotiating an offer.
- Reviewing a resume for someone else.

**Honesty rule:** never invent a title, employer, date, degree, certification or metric. Improve the framing of true work; do not manufacture work. Fabrication fails at reference check and is career-ending.

## Stage 1 - Target definition

Before touching the resume, define:

1. **Role title** exactly as the market names it (not your internal title).
2. **Seniority** - the level you can defend in an interview, not the one you want.
3. **3 target companies** and 10 more like them.
4. **The one sentence** that makes you interesting for that role: domain + skill + proof.
5. **Constraints** - location, remote, visa, notice period, salary floor. Write them down; they end up in negotiation.

Applying to 5 well-matched roles beats 100 generic ones, and the data has said so for a decade.

## Stage 2 - ATS-proof resume structure

- **One page** under 8 years of experience, two pages maximum after.
- Format: `.docx` or a text-based PDF. No tables for layout, no columns, no text boxes, no headers/footers, no images, no icons.
- Contact line: name, city, phone, email, LinkedIn, portfolio. No photo, no date of birth, no marital status, no full address.
- Sections in order: Summary (2 lines, optional) → Skills → Experience → Projects → Education → Certifications.
- Dates right-aligned as `MM/YYYY – MM/YYYY`. Gaps: one line of explanation if over 6 months, none if under.
- File name: `Firstname-Lastname-Role.pdf`.

## Stage 3 - Bullets that survive screening

Format: **Action verb + what + how + measurable result**.

> Weak: "Responsible for improving the checkout page."
> Strong: "Rebuilt checkout in React, cutting step count from 5 to 3 and lifting completion 12% over one quarter."

Rules:

- Start with a verb: built, led, reduced, migrated, automated, negotiated. Never "responsible for" or "worked on".
- One metric per bullet where it truthfully exists: %, ₹, time, count, users, latency.
- 3-5 bullets for the current role, 2-3 for older ones. Most recent gets the most space.
- Mirror the job description's vocabulary for skills you genuinely have (ATS matches keywords literally; "JS" does not match "JavaScript").
- No adjectives about yourself ("hardworking", "passionate"). Show it.

## Stage 4 - Tailoring per application

For each application, change exactly four things: the summary line, the skills order, 2-3 bullets to emphasise the matching work, and the file name. Twenty minutes per application, not two.

Keep a `master.md` with every true achievement and its metrics. Cut from it per role; never rewrite from scratch.

## Stage 5 - Portfolio proof

The resume says it; the portfolio proves it. For each target role, one artefact:

- Engineer: a deployed project with a README that states the problem, the approach, the trade-offs and the result.
- Designer: 3 case studies in problem → process → decision → outcome form, not a gallery of screenshots.
- Marketer: one campaign with numbers, one writing sample, one teardown.
- Analyst: one dashboard or notebook with a clear question and a defensible answer.

Live link > screenshots > claims. Never link a dead URL; check every link a week before applying.

## Stage 6 - Outreach that gets replies

- Find a person, not a portal: the hiring manager or a team member, not `careers@`.
- 90 words maximum: who you are (one line), a specific reason for *their* company (one line, must be un-fakeable), the proof (one line with a link), the ask (a 15-minute call, or "is this the right person?").
- No attachments on first contact. No "I am passionate about".
- Follow up once after 4 working days, then stop.
- Track: company, contact, date, channel, status, next action. A spreadsheet beats memory.

## Stage 7 - Interview prep

1. **The 6 stories.** Prepare STAR stories that cover: a hard technical decision, a conflict, a failure and the lesson, a delivered result with a number, leading without authority, and learning something fast. These cover 80% of behavioural questions.
2. **Company research** - product, business model, recent news, the team's public work. Two specific observations you can offer.
3. **Role-specific drills** - 10 problems in your domain, out loud, timed.
4. **Questions to ask** - about the team's biggest constraint, how success is measured in 90 days, and what happened to the last person in this role.
5. **Mock once with a person**, recorded if possible. Watching yourself is unpleasant and effective.

## Stage 8 - Negotiation

- Never give the first number if you can avoid it; if forced, give a researched range with the floor at your true floor.
- Research: levels.fyi, Glassdoor, peer conversations. In India, also check the band the role is posted at and the city differential.
- Negotiate the whole package: base, variable, equity, joining bonus, notice-period buyout, remote days, learning budget, title, review date.
- Script: enthusiasm → the ask → the reason (market data, scope) → silence. Do not fill the silence.
- Get the final offer in writing before resigning. Always.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] One page, text-based, no tables or columns
- [ ] Every bullet has a verb and, where true, a number
- [ ] Keywords mirror the job description honestly
- [ ] Four things tailored per application
- [ ] Portfolio link live and relevant
- [ ] Outreach under 90 words, one follow-up
- [ ] Six STAR stories rehearsed
- [ ] Salary range researched, offer in writing
- [ ] Nothing on the resume that cannot be defended

## Copy-ready agent prompt

> Read the skill at the URL above. I will give you my current resume and a target job description. Rewrite it to the Stage 2 structure, convert every bullet to the Stage 3 format using only facts I give you - ask me for the missing metrics instead of inventing them - and produce the tailored version plus a 90-word outreach message and the six STAR stories you would expect to be asked. Flag every claim you cannot verify.
